\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have added a single bilinear term, $\Delta\mathcal{H}=\Delta n\,H_{jkl}
\varepsilon_{jk,l}$, with its two thermodynamic reciprocals, to the
flexoelectric piezoelectric-semiconductor continuum of a $6mm$ ZnO plate, and we
have computed how a guided mode is scattered at a free edge under that coupling.
The conclusions are the following.

The scattering problem is well posed and its energy budget closes.  At the
reference plane the incident power is reproduced by the sum of the outgoing
self-powers, the interference term and the conduction loss to
$3.4\times10^{-16}$, and the solver's own power balance to
$7.3\times10^{-13}$.  This closes the energy identity that the earlier version
of this work left open, where a wrongly propagated evanescent remainder of order
$10^{-5}$ had been read as a physical reactive contribution.

The edge converts a measurable share of the incident guided mode into a
backward-propagating branch.  At the operating point
($k_xd=0.7362$, $k_yd=0.1298$, \SI{16.68}{\giga\hertz},
$d=\SI{200}{\nano\metre}$) the backward channel carries \SI{2.31}{\percent} of
the incident power, leaving the reference plane at
$-27.9^{\circ}$; the maximum along the branch is
\SI{57.9}{\percent} at $k_xd=0.55$, where the branch is slowest and
$v_g\to0$.  Both values are converged to better than $10^{-6}$ in relative terms
between Galerkin degree 18 and degree 30, and both are resolved projections with
$|\cos\theta_E|$ of \SI{88}{\percent} and \SI{97}{\percent}.

These numbers are shares of the scattered power and not reflectances.  The
total outgoing propagating power is \SI{99.99}{\percent} of the incident power
at the operating point and \SI{99.88}{\percent} at the peak, so the plate is an
almost perfect scatterer of the guided mode at both.  Negative reflection here
means that one specific outgoing branch carries energy in $-x$; it does not
mean that the edge returns most of the power.

The added coupling is a perturbation of the partition and a strong modifier of
the loss.  Across $|H|\le2.0\times10^{-23}\,\si{\joule\metre}$ the backward
channel moves by at most \SI{2.1}{\percent} and the dispersion by less than
$4\times10^{-6}$ in relative terms, while the attenuation length changes by a
factor of sixty-two.  The transverse wavenumber, by contrast, changes the
backward channel by a factor of $1.35$ across the solved box.  The ordering
$H\ll k_y$ in the control of the partition is the quantitative content of the
statement that this coupling is not the organising parameter of the effect.

The formulation is a Fourier-reduced three-dimensional one: three displacement
components, a single conserved $k_y$, and full strain-gradient kinematics.  The
electrical boundary conditions are $\varphi=0$ for the grounded case and
$D_z=0$ for the open-circuit case, with blocking carrier conditions on all free
surfaces and no free charge on the reflecting edge; the labels in the earlier
version of this manuscript were reversed and are corrected here.

The conclusions are bounded by the limitations of \cref{sec:disc_limitations}.
The strain-gradient length, the flexoelectric coefficient and the coupling
tensor are synthetic, the carrier physics is linearised about a uniform doping,
the plate is semi-infinite with a single edge, and the relation of the added
coupling to the published flexoelectric-semiconductor constitutive relations
remains unresolved.  Within those limits, the results show that the
carrier--strain-gradient coupling is a controlled and reproducible modifier of
an edge-scattering partition, and that the partition itself --- a slow-mode
effect with a peak exceeding half of the incident power --- is the phenomenon
worth measuring.
