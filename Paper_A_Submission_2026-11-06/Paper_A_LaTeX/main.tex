\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Flexoelectricity converts a strain gradient into an electric polarisation and,
in a piezoelectric semiconductor, the resulting charge redistribution modifies
the carrier density that screens it.  We examine what happens when this
two-way exchange is allowed to act on a guided elastic plate mode that
propagates towards a free edge.  The model is a transversely isotropic
($6mm$) n-type ZnO plate of thickness $d$, described by strain-gradient
elasticity, piezoelectricity, flexoelectricity and linearised drift--diffusion
of the electron gas, and the sole constitutive addition is the bilinear
carrier--strain-gradient enthalpy $\Delta\mathcal{H}=\Delta n\,H_{jkl}
\varepsilon_{jk,l}$ together with its reciprocal partners
$\Delta\tau_{jkl}=H_{jkl}\Delta n$ and $\Delta\widetilde\mu=H_{jkl}
\varepsilon_{jk,l}$.  The plate occupies the half-space $x<0$ and terminates
at a traction-free edge at $x=0$; the fields carry a single conserved
tangential wavenumber $k_y$ and are discretised through the thickness with a
spectral Galerkin expansion, which turns the edge problem into a linear
matching of outgoing modes.
Two results are established with an explicit refinement study rather than by
assertion.  First, at the operating point ($k_xd=0.736$, $k_yd=0.130$,
\SI{16.68}{\giga\hertz}) the backward-propagating channel carries \SI{2.31}{\percent} of the incident
power, and this value is stable to $7\times10^{-7}$ under refinement of the
Galerkin degree from 18 to 30.
Second, along the same guided branch the backward channel captures up to
\SI{57.92}{\percent} of the incident power near $k_xd=0.55$, where the group velocity of
the branch passes through zero; this maximum is stable to
$1.5\times10^{-7}$ and is well resolved (energy angle $-14.4^{\circ}$,
$|\cos\theta_E|=0.97$).  Both numbers are \emph{modal partitions} of the
outgoing flux and not total reflectances: at the operating point the total
outgoing propagating power is $0.99985$ of the incident power, so the remaining
propagating outgoing branches carry the difference; near the peak that total is
$0.9988$.  The carrier--strain-gradient
coupling $H$ shifts the backward channel by at most \SI{2.4}{\percent}, whereas
the transverse wavenumber $k_y$ changes it by a factor of 1.35 over the solved
box; the effect reported here is therefore a slow-mode edge effect that the
coupling modulates, not a resonance of the coupling term alone.  The energy
budget is closed on the exact identity
$P_{\text{inc}}+\sum_jP_j+P_{\text{cross}}=P_{\text{edge}}-P_{\text{diss}}$
with a residual of $7\times10^{-13}$, and the reactive content of the
evanescent spectrum is shown to decay below $10^{-25}$ at the reference plane,
so no reactive term is counted as transported energy.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} flexoelectricity; piezoelectric semiconductor;
strain-gradient elasticity; guided waves; negative reflection; drift--diffusion;
spectral Galerkin method; edge scattering.
\end{abstract}
