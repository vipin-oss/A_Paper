## 1. Gate 1 — generalised traction, double traction, surface divergence, corner force

**What was wrong at the start of the round.** The traction had never been
*verified*: the only statement in the record was that the discrete flux row "is the
traction-like flux conjugate to the displacement state". An attempt to check it
during this round failed by $5\times10^{-1}$, and the reason turned out to be
substantive rather than cosmetic.

**Derivation** (`PAPER_A_ROUND21_GATE1_DERIVATION.md`, §1–2). From
$\delta\Pi_{\text{mech}}$ with two integrations by parts in the volume and one on
the face:

$$
t_j=n_i(\sigma_{ij}-\partial_k\tau_{ijk})-\partial^s_i(n_k\tau_{jik}),\qquad
m_j=n_in_k\tau_{ijk},\qquad
f_j=\nu_in_k\tau_{jik},
$$

with two index facts that the manuscript had wrong and that only the verification
could settle: the free index of the surface-divergence term is the **first** index
of the hyperstress, and its **gradient** index is the face normal. Thus
$t_j=\Sigma_{j1}-ik_y\tau_{jy1}-\partial_z\tau_{jz1}$ on the edge $x=0$, not
$\partial_j^s\tau_{ixj}$ as the manuscript previously had it. The manuscript is
corrected (`sections/governing_equations.tex`, new
Eq. `eq:generalised_traction`).

**Second, independent evaluation.** Mode by mode, on the *complete* incident +
reflected field, the discrete rows are compared with the derived fields:

| check | value |
|---|---|
| $\langle T_u\rangle$ vs $\langle t\rangle$ + corner functional, incident mode, degree 12 | **$1.4\times10^{-13}$** |
| same at degrees 10, 16, 20, 28 | $9.4\times10^{-14}$–$1.3\times10^{-12}$ |
| worst reflected (evanescent) mode, degrees 8–16 | $8.9\times10^{-9}$–$3.5\times10^{-6}$ |
| same with the corner term dropped | $1.7\times10^{1}$ |
| same keeping only $\tau_{j31}$ | $5.7\times10^{-1}$ |
| same with $\tau_{j31}-\tau_{j13}$ | $1.1\times10^{0}$ |
| $\langle R\rangle$ vs $\langle\tau_{j11}\rangle$ | $1.7\times10^{-7}$ (worst mode), $10^{-13}$ (incident) |

The identity is exact to round-off for the physical mode and is the check that
fixed the index placement and the sign of the corner term instead of accepting
them. A defect in the earlier attempt (`O(n_quad^{-2})`) was traced to evaluating
corner values at the nearest Gauss node rather than at $z=\pm\frac12$; the endpoint
basis is now used. Evidence: `figure_data/round21/gate1_traction_identity.json`.

**Difference between the two evaluations, quantified and stated in the paper.**
The double traction is annihilated pointwise ($2\times10^{-13}$ on the complete
field). The traction is not, and cannot be: a Galerkin statement imposes natural
data as moments, and the corner datum is $\delta$-like. The residual of the
complete field is $9.4\times10^{-4}$ of the incident scale at the corrected maximum
and $3.6\times10^{-2}$ at the operating point, and it does **not** decrease with
degree. That is a property of the weak solution, quoted in the manuscript
(`sections/numerical_methods.tex`, sixth verification item, and
`sections/discussion.tex`, limitation 2) rather than suppressed.

## 2. Gate 2 — the corner line force: **bounded closure**

The corner force is `f_i = τ_{i31} + τ_{i13}`, a load-bearing part of the natural
data (§1) that no polynomial basis can set to zero pointwise. It is therefore
measured in three configurations, seven degrees and three quadratures on the
complete field (`figure_data/round21/gate12_corner.json`):

| configuration | $f/(f_{\text{inc}})$ at $n=10$ | at $n=28$ | fitted law | exponent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| operating point | $8.42\times10^{-2}$ | $4.46\times10^{-2}$ | $3.51\times10^{-1}n^{-0.618}$ | $-0.618$ |
| corrected maximum | $3.27\times10^{-3}$ | $1.71\times10^{-3}$ | $1.41\times10^{-2}n^{-0.632}$ | $-0.632$ |
| $k_xd=0.55$ | $3.79\times10^{-2}$ | $1.98\times10^{-2}$ | $1.63\times10^{-1}n^{-0.630}$ | $-0.630$ |

* One algebraic law with the **same exponent to 0.014** in three independent
  configurations — the signature of an unresolved corner singularity of the
  continuum field, not of a coding or quadrature artefact.
* Quadrature: $f=2.92997\times10^{-3}$ at $n_{\text{quad}}=96,128,192$ — identical to
  six digits.
* Three corner-localisation scales: at $n=28$, band maxima exceed the pointwise
  corner value by factors 5–10 (e.g. $0.425$ vs $0.0446$ at the operating point), so
  no band average can argue the corner datum away, and none is used.
* Not load-bearing: the reported partition at the maximum moves by
  $3.9\times10^{-6}$ between degrees 10 and 28 while $f$ halves; the energy identity
  closes to $10^{-12}$; $f$ carries no power in this formulation.

**What is not claimed**: that the corner force is zero, or that the exponent is
provably $0.62$; that would need the local corner eigenvalue problem. The label is
*bounded closure*: quantified, decaying, bounded over the tested range, and
demonstrably decoupled from every reported number. The scattering result is
therefore **not** scientifically conditional on it. This replaces the round-20
position ("not resolved pointwise") in the manuscript.

## 7. Gate 7 — the identity of DOI 10.1016/j.ijengsci.2024.104165

Verified from the publisher's record (Elsevier landing page, abstract and keyword
list): **Y. Qu, E. Pan, F. Zhu, Q. Deng, "A thermodynamically consistent theory for
flexoelectronics: Interaction between strain gradient and electric current in
flexoelectric semiconductors", Int. J. Eng. Sci. 208 (2025) 104165.** The abstract
establishes that the article derives the theory from mass/charge/momentum/angular
momentum balance with the thermodynamic laws, obtains constitutive equations by
the Coleman–Noll procedure including piezoelectric, flexoelectric, thermoelectric
and drift–diffusion couplings, sets up a principle of virtual work for the
strain-gradient semiconducting continuum, and linearises it for small carrier
perturbations. The full text is closed access and was not read.

**Consequence, applied to the manuscript**: a carrier–strain-gradient coupling of
the class written here is *not* new to that framework, and no priority is claimed
for the $H$ term. `sections/discussion.tex` now states what the published record
does establish, and confines the comparison of equations to the open-access
reproduction of the free-energy decomposition that *was* inspected. The
contribution is located where it belongs: the edge-scattering problem, the
backward branch and the partition, which exist with $H=0$.

## 8. Gate 8 — provenance of ℓ, f and H

Unchanged and explicit in the manuscript: $\ell=50$ nm, $f_0=1$ nC/m and
$H^*$ are **synthetic representative values chosen to place the effect in a
measurable range** — never described as measured, never attributed to a material
datasheet. The manuscript's limitations state the sensitivity ($\ell$ controls the
existence of the backward branch; $f$ is neutral at the 0.01 % level in the
partition; $H$ moves it by 1.2 %, 10.2 % and 36.1 % at the three sampled
configurations) and the scope limit (the strain-gradient length must be a
non-negligible fraction of the thickness for the branch to exist at all).
